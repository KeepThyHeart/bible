Provenance
==========

Why each text and tune in this library is in the public domain, and which
edition it was transcribed from.

This file is not optional. It is the whole defence of the library's licensing
posture, and it is enormously cheaper to record as each hymn is added than to
reconstruct for four hundred files later.

The trap to keep in mind: a hymn text can be public domain while a particular
*translation*, *arrangement*, or modernised wording of it is not. Watts and
Wesley are safe; a 1985 hymnal's updated pronouns and edited third stanza are a
new work. Record which edition each text came from, and prefer a dated scan over
a lyrics site.

United States rule of thumb used here: anything published before 1929 is in the
public domain. Every entry below clears that by a wide margin. Where a text and
its tune have different dates, both are given, because they are separate works.


amazing-grace
-------------

  * Text: John Newton, published in `Olney Hymns`, Book I, Hymn 41 (London,
    1779), under the title "Faith's Review and Expectation". Published 1779;
    Newton died 1807. Public domain everywhere.
  * Tune: NEW BRITAIN, an anonymous American melody first printed in
    `Virginia Harmony` (1831) and paired with this text in
    `Southern Harmony` (1835). Anonymous and pre-1929.
  * **Note on verse 5.** "When we've been there ten thousand years" is not
    Newton's. It is an anonymous stanza, in circulation by the early nineteenth
    century and printed with this text from 1852, and is itself public domain.
    It is included because it is universally sung as part of this hymn, and
    recorded here because a library that silently attributes it to Newton is
    wrong even when the licensing is safe.


tis-so-sweet-to-trust-in-jesus
------------------------------

  * Text: Louisa M. R. Stead, 1882, first published in `Songs of Triumph`
    (Philadelphia, 1882). Stead died 1917.
  * Tune: TRUST IN JESUS, William J. Kirkpatrick, 1882, published with the text.
    Kirkpatrick died 1921.


my-jesus-i-love-thee
--------------------

  * Text: William R. Featherstone, written about 1862 and first published
    anonymously in `The London Hymn Book` (London, 1864). Featherstone died 1873.
  * Tune: GORDON, Adoniram J. Gordon, published in `The Vestry Hymn and Tune
    Book` (1872) and widely from 1876. Gordon died 1895.


it-is-well-with-my-soul
-----------------------

  * Text: Horatio G. Spafford, written 1873, first published in
    `Gospel Hymns No. 2` (1876). Spafford died 1888.
  * Tune: VILLE DU HAVRE, Philip P. Bliss, 1876, published with the text. Bliss
    died in December 1876.


Considered and excluded
-----------------------

Recorded so the same question is not researched twice.

  * **Victory in Jesus** -- Eugene M. Bartlett, 1939. Copyright renewed 1967 and
    assigned to Albert E. Brumley & Sons; it remains under copyright in the
    United States until 2035. It is a mid-twentieth-century gospel song, not an
    old hymn, and it cannot be admitted here whatever it feels like.

    This is not a gap to be filled later. A congregation that wants to project
    it should do so under its own CCLI or comparable licence, using the
    presenter's **custom text** item -- which exists for exactly this case, and
    which keeps the licence obligation with the person who actually holds the
    licence.
